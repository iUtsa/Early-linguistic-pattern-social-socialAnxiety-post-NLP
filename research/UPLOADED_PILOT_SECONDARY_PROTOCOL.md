# Fixed secondary pilot checks

Written on 7 October 2026 while the two primary runs were executing, before inspecting their test metrics. This addition is exploratory, not preregistered. It does not change either primary cohort or fitted model. The original protocol and its recorded hash remain intact.

1. **Length/activity restriction.** Construct a balanced June subset separately for each comparison. Strata use an author's mean whitespace word count over cleaned title/body text with fixed boundaries 0, 50, 100, 150, 250, 500, 1000, infinity; activity is 1, 2 or 3+ eligible posts. Keep the smaller class count in each stratum from BOTH classes, ordered by SHA-256 of seed 42 and observed account identifier. Selection uses label and covariates only, never predictions or correctness. Report total/class retention, matching strata, residual standardized mean differences in length/activity and the changed 50% prevalence. Evaluate all six frozen primary models at the same fixed threshold. Give 1,000-author bootstrap intervals for the 13-feature and TF-IDF models and their paired macro-F1 contrast. Coarse matching does not exactly equalize continuous covariates and changes the target population; it is a sensitivity analysis, not a causal adjustment or clinical validation.

2. **Frozen lexical-model keyword sensitivity.** Recompute TF-IDF predictions on all primary June authors after replacing the original 16 leak terms plus `depression`, `depressed`, `depressive`, `mentalhealth`, `suicidewatch`, and `subreddit` with `term` before concatenating each author's text. Keep the vectorizer, model and threshold fixed. Report the finite term list, metric changes, paired intervals and anonymous prediction artifact hash. This test does not remove all topic language and is not masked retraining. It differs deliberately from the primary 13-feature sensitivity's original term list; do not treat the masks as identical conditions or rank their drops as directly comparable effects.

No additional C tuning, outcome-driven matching bins, clinical labels, demographic inference or prefix analysis is introduced. The two comparisons share authors; they remain dependent analyses of the supplied examples.
