## Title

Linguistic and lexical models for anxiety-community language: a temporal robustness study in RMHD

## Abstract

Community-derived mental-health labels can conflate writing context with psychological constructs. We compare 13 sentiment, pronoun and style features with TF-IDF lexical content for anxiety-community classification in Reddit Mental Health Dataset version 1. Observed-account models train in March 2022, tune in May and evaluate unseen June accounts; frozen models subsequently evaluate July and August. June comparisons against mentalhealth and depression contain 8,717 and 9,622 accounts. At May-selected thresholds, linguistic logistic regression achieves macro-F1 0.594 and 0.641, versus 0.859 and 0.921 for TF-IDF. Paired lexical advantages are 0.265 [95% account-bootstrap interval 0.253–0.276] and 0.280 [0.270–0.289]. Nonlinear models using the same linguistic features reach 0.613 and 0.659. Cross-comparator application reduces both representations' discrimination on identical target cohorts. Balanced length/activity restrictions retain lexical advantages; finite disorder/community-term perturbations reduce lexical performance. Across four later-period cohorts, frozen-model lexical advantages remain 0.263–0.286 and persist under stricter observed-history exclusions. Twenty training-account resamples per comparison retain positive lexical advantages in every run. These exploratory results demonstrate useful but weaker predictive signal in the specified small representation and short-term persistence of the gap within one collection. They do not establish diagnosis, early onset, causal psychological mechanisms or external generalization. Code, cohort rules, protocols, source hashes and aggregate evidence are available without redistributing individual sensitive material.
