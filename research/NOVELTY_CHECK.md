# Novelty check and experiment decision

Assessment and retrieval date: **7 October 2026**. This evaluates the replacement community-language study, not the unsupported early/social-anxiety claims in arXiv:2601.11758v1. Search queries, coverage, source hashes and reading status are recorded in [novelty_search_manifest.json](novelty_search_manifest.json).

## Decision

**I would proceed to an appropriately scoped applied submission after the authors complete their factual declarations. I do not identify another experiment as necessary to support the replacement's narrow claims.** The completed account-level comparisons, operating-point check, nonlinear control, same-target transfer, length/activity restriction, term perturbations, reuse audit, frozen July/August evaluation and training-account resampling provide enough evidence to assess those claims. This is a research judgment, not a journal's acceptance decision or a claim that reviewers cannot request more work.

**The broad idea already exists.** Anxiety-language classification, linguistic-versus-lexical comparisons, control-corpus sensitivity, temporal/domain transfer, topical restrictions and proxy-label criticism have substantial precedents. Calling any of these the first discovery would be incorrect. No exact duplicate of the *complete* RMHD account-cohort design and measured results was identified in the inspected literature. That is a bounded search finding; it neither certifies worldwide uniqueness nor makes the combination of routine checks a new method.

The defensible contribution is an **empirical replication and extension in an audited dataset release**, documenting the size and short-term stability of a particular representation gap. Its main rejection risk is that editors or reviewers find this contribution too incremental. An applied Q4 route is the most defensible starting point in the earlier venue assessment; Q3 is scope-dependent, and the evidence does not support prioritizing Q2. Quartiles are ranking/category information, not scientific eligibility rules. The [submission decision](SUBMISSION_DECISION.md) contains the underlying findings and remaining human-author requirements.

## What was searched

1. **Crossref:** 16 distinct bibliographic queries, retrieving the top 30 records each, filtered to publication dates from 2010 through 7 October 2026. The 480 returned records contain **382 unique DOIs**. Queries cover the source article title, RMHD/Kaggle owner, anxiety, mentalhealth/depression comparators, sentiment/pronouns, TF-IDF, temporal/user splits, masking and confounding. Titles and available abstracts were screened; selected records and references led to closer reading. Crossref queries are relevance-ranked, not exhaustive term matches. Many results are irrelevant, including physics uses of “RMHD”; 382 is a retrieval-pool count, not a count of relevant studies read.
2. **ACL Anthology:** the complete bibliography-plus-abstracts snapshot contains **131,647 entries**. An automated filter for mental-health terms plus Reddit/social-media/domain/generalization/control terms retained **432 candidates**. Selected titles/abstracts and references were examined, and close papers were read in full or in the specified sections. These counts describe metadata screening, not full-text reading of 131,647 or 432 papers. The filter and snapshot hash are recorded for reproducibility.
3. **arXiv:** five metadata queries covered the dataset phrase, source-author/title terms and anxiety/linguistic/comparator language. One broad query returned only its first 30 of 422 matches; the other four returned all available matches under their caps. Selected preprints' dataset descriptions were checked. Search fields and query syntax affect recall; metadata queries do not search every paper's full body.
4. **Targeted reading and reference follow-up:** this extends the earlier reading of SMHD, RSDD, Ireland and Iserman, Harrigian and colleagues, anxiety/panic comparisons and measurement-validity work. The table below distinguishes established findings from this study's remaining increment.

OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and Bing requests were blocked by the runtime network proxy and contributed no usable results. Some JMIR/PMC, Research Square, Nature and SSRN full-text/landing requests were also blocked. Their accessible Crossref metadata are explicitly marked below. No Scopus or Web of Science citation search was performed. This is a substantial targeted novelty audit, **not an exhaustive systematic review or proof of absence**. Newly indexed papers, inaccessible texts and poorly indexed datasets can still contain closer overlap.

## Closest work and the overlap that matters

Scores from different papers are not comparable experiments: datasets, outcomes, prevalence and model features differ.

| Prior work | What is already established | What remains different in this study |
|---|---|---|
| [Shen & Rudzicz, 2017, *Detecting Anxiety through Reddit*](https://aclanthology.org/W17-3107/) | Anxiety-related versus general narrative Reddit posts; lexicon, n-gram, topic and embedding features. Anxiety-language representation comparisons substantially predate this paper. | Exclusive observed accounts in the specified RMHD release, adjacent mental-health comparison communities and later unseen-account windows. No new anxiety classifier is claimed. |
| [Ireland & Iserman, 2018, *Within and Between-Person Differences in Language Used Across Anxiety Support and Neutral Reddit Communities*](https://aclanthology.org/W18-0620/) | Anxiety-support members' language within/outside support contexts, matched comparison users, held-out-user classification and word/dictionary features. Context discrimination and member discrimination are distinct tasks. | Adjacent mental-health comparators, a fixed 13-feature versus lexical representation and an audited short-term extension. Context-sensitive anxiety language is already established. |
| [Pirina & Çöltekin, 2018, *Identifying Depression on Reddit: The Effect of Training Data*](https://aclanthology.org/W18-5903/) | Alternative depression/control training corpora, lexical classifiers, one post per author and evaluation on a common target test set. | [Harrigian, Aguirre & Dredze, 2020, *Do Models of Mental Health Based on Social Media Data Generalize?*](https://aclanthology.org/2020.findings-emnlp.337/) | Five depression datasets, lexical/LIWC/topic/embedding representations, temporal and cross-platform transfer, and confounding concerns. | A smaller within-source case with two adjacent negative communities and a measured gap that persists under specific controls. This does not discover domain shift or establish broader generalization. |
| [Wolohan et al., 2018, *Detecting Linguistic Traces of Depression in Topic-Restricted Text*](https://aclanthology.org/W18-4102/) and [Pananookooln et al., 2023, *Comparing Selective Masking Methods for Depression Detection in Social Media*](https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-3.1/) | Topic-restricted linguistic/lexical prediction and comparisons of seven selective masking strategies, including pretraining/fine-tuning and class-imbalance choices. | A frozen finite-list term perturbation on different account cohorts. | [Sharma & Sirts, 2024, *Context is Important in Depressive Language*](https://aclanthology.org/2024.wassa-1.28/) | Topics change the interpretation of sentiment and linguistic markers in self-reported-depression Reddit data. | A predictive representation comparison for community affiliation. Sentiment/pronoun interpretation is not a validated anxiety mechanism. |
| [Hassan, 2026, *The Divergence Hypothesis: Unmasking Lexical Interference and Label Bias in Mental Health NLP*](https://aclanthology.org/2026.bionlp-1.1/) | A lexical, morphosyntactic and 154-feature psycholinguistic audit across four human/proxy-labeled datasets, masking, paired inference and label-source/generalization analyses. The correction of an invalid original claim is necessary, but is not itself scientific novelty. |

The closest conceptual challenges are **Pirina & Çöltekin, Harrigian and colleagues, and Hassan**. Ignoring them could make the contribution look larger than it is. They are now explicitly reflected in the manuscript's positioning and citations.

## Same dataset name and unresolved recent candidates

“Reddit Mental Health Dataset,” “RMHD” or “Kaggle” alone does not identify the same collection. The present inputs are checksum-verified **Rani, Ahmed & Subramani's version-1 release**, collected January 2019–August 2022, dataset ID 3687885 and owner `entenam`. Its five authored communities differ from several other widely reused collections.

- [Low et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.2196/22635) uses the name RMHD for a **different 2018–2020 collection** involving 15 mental-health and 11 comparison communities. Its abstract already describes community classification and interpretable text features. Here only its metadata/abstract were accessible; its complete protocol was not independently compared from full text.
- [Shao, Zhu & Zhai, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05075) identifies a roughly 700,000-row, four-class 2022 Kaggle resource and cites `kamaruladha/mental-disorders-identification-reddit-nlp`. [Ahmed, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07653) describes a similarly titled collection with posts from 2012–2022 and cites the same Kaggle owner/resource. Their dataset sections concern a different source/design from the present release; the shared generic name does not establish duplication.
- [Ogundunmade, 2026, SSRN preprint](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6788301), *Comparative Machine Learning Models for Mental Health Prediction on Reddit with SHAP Analysis*, describes Kaggle data, linguistic features, sentiment, TF-IDF, LR/Naive Bayes/SVC/XGBoost and SHAP. **Only Crossref metadata/abstract were reviewed.** Exact dataset version, author separation, temporal policy and the full experiment cannot be verified from that abstract. It is a close model-ingredient candidate, not evidence that our complete study is duplicated or that it is unique. The deposited publication year has no month/day precision.
- [Sánchez Rodríguez et al., 2025, Research Square preprint](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-6392473/v2), *Mental Health on Reddit: Explainable Classification with Transformers*, describes four condition communities, BERT/MentalBERT and LIME. The Crossref version relation leads to the published **[Sánchez Rodríguez et al., 2026, *Understanding mental health discourse on Reddit with transformers and explainability*, Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-35918-3)**, published 31 January 2026. The preprint's metadata/abstract and the journal article's metadata/references were reviewed; neither full text was accessible, and the journal record supplies no abstract. Exact release/cohort identity and any revisions remain unverified. The preprint's stated model/outcome is insufficient to rule out every overlapping analysis in the inaccessible published text. This is a published precedent, not merely an unpublished candidate.
- Additional metadata candidates include [Wagay & Jahiruddin, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procs.2025.04.398), [Senanayake & Liang, 2025](https://doi.org/10.5220/0013375900003911) and [Ayyalasomayajula et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.11591/ijece.v14i5.pp5998-6005). They reinforce that Reddit condition prediction, anxiety emotion analysis and TF-IDF/logistic regression are established. The first two lack abstracts in the retrieved records; none was treated as a full-text protocol comparison.

No inspected full text reports this entire combination: the identified RMHD version; the two exclusive observed-account anxiety contrasts; March training and May tuning; held-out June and frozen July/August account windows; the fixed 13-feature versus lexical comparison; and the reported operating-point, nonlinear, identical-target, covariate/reuse and training-resampling results. **Absence of that combination is a narrower observation than originality of its scientific ideas.** Exact replication on the same data would not automatically prevent publication either; the relevant question is whether the empirical replication/extension is useful and accurately positioned.

## Reviewer judgment and what would actually require new data

| Reviewer question | Present answer | Further experiment needed for the stated paper? |
|---|---|---|
| Are labels anxiety diagnoses or early onset? | No. The title, estimand and conclusions now state observed community affiliation. | No, if claims remain bounded. Validated anxiety outcomes and a separate design are necessary to restore a clinical/onset claim. |
| Is the small-feature deficit just threshold or linear-model choice? | May-selected thresholds and a tuned histogram-boosting control improve the small model but retain a large lexical advantage. | No additional model is identified as necessary for this question. A transformer is not required to compare these two representations. |
| Does overlap or account leakage explain the result? | Observed-account separation, exact-text quarantine and a defined near-reuse audit are documented; later historical-absence subsets retain the gap. | No further experiment is identified as necessary for the specified leakage claim. Semantic duplication and unobserved account identity remain limitations. |
| Is this one lucky fit or one June cohort? | Four later frozen cohorts and 20 training-account resamples per comparison retain the gap. | No. These checks are already completed, although they are within-source and short-term. |
| Is comparator choice causally responsible? | Source cohorts/hyperparameters change with comparators; the paper describes dependence on the fitted comparison, not an isolated causal effect. | No, for that descriptive statement. A more controlled design would be needed for a causal attribution. |
| Does it establish external or clinical generalization? | No. | An independent compatible source or validated outcome is necessary for those broader claims, not for the current case study. |
| Is the contribution sufficiently novel? | It is incremental and explicitly framed as a replication/extension. | More classifiers on the same benchmark would not settle this. Independent replication or a genuinely new scientific question could strengthen it, but a suitable applied venue can assess the current contribution. |

**Recommended stopping point:** keep the current experiments and bounded interpretation, complete the private author/ethics statements and target-journal formatting, and submit to a venue whose scope accepts applied replication or robustness work. Do not add experiments simply to lengthen the model table. If the target demands a general methodological advance or cross-source inference, change the study ambition and obtain appropriate independent evidence; the current manuscript does not meet that stronger objective.

The manuscript title, introduction, discussion and related-work citations have been revised accordingly. The search is documented openly so a reviewer can assess both the contribution and the remaining coverage limits.
