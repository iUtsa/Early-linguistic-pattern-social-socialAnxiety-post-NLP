# Elsevier journal fit for the revised RMHD paper

Assessment date: **7 October 2026**. Paper assessed: **Linguistic and lexical models for anxiety-community language: a temporal robustness study in RMHD**, using the current [manuscript abstract](REVISED_PILOT_MANUSCRIPT.md). This assessment concerns the completed community-language study, not an early-onset or diagnostic anxiety paper. Evidence and access status are recorded in [elsevier_journal_search_manifest.json](elsevier_journal_search_manifest.json).

## Recommendation

**Natural Language Processing Journal is the strongest Elsevier thematic fit I identified.** Its scope reproduced by SCImago explicitly invites scientific replication studies, emphasizes trustworthy/interpretable language research and promotes reproducibility. I would consider one submission there if an Elsevier venue is a priority and its live requirements and funding arrangements are acceptable. It is **SJR 2025 Q1**, so this is a scope-based, ambitious option rather than a safe lower-tier placement.

**Social Sciences & Humanities Open** is a plausible second option if the discussion clearly explains the significance of community-derived labels and context for social-science measurement. **Informatics in Medicine Unlocked** is a conditional technical alternative, with a stronger medical-informatics relevance objection. **Franklin Open** is a secondary technical backup with an engineering/applied-mathematics scope risk. I would put **Machine Learning with Applications** behind these for the present manuscript: its scope explicitly requires novelty and practical value, and routine model comparison can be insufficient.

I found **no convincing Q3/Q4 Elsevier fallback in the candidate set examined**. That is not a claim that none exists across the publisher's entire catalogue. A medically oriented journal's Q3 category does not repair the absence of a medical outcome, and an engineering category cannot be used to establish computer-science Q2 eligibility. The conservative applied Q4 route outside Elsevier described in the earlier [submission assessment](SUBMISSION_DECISION.md) remains reasonable if minimizing speculative submissions is the priority.

This search does not strengthen the scientific novelty of the paper. The contribution remains an incremental, single-source robustness case. Replication-friendly scopes provide a legitimate reason to consider particular journals despite that limitation; they provide no acceptance guarantee or requirement that an editor send the manuscript for review.
